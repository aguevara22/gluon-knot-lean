## 2. `lp:coefficient-transport` re-derived independently (rule 8, cross-check)

The lemma is a campaign step. I re-derived its proof from the printed text
without consulting the Codex packet or the earlier verdicts:

- **Coordinate split.** `1, i` is a free `Z`-basis of `Z[i]`, so `G_D = U_D + iV_D` with `U, V ∈ T` is unique; the skein's coefficients `l, l^{-1}, m` lie in `T`, so comparing coordinates gives the source skein separately for `U` and for `V`, with `U_○ = 1`, `V_○ = 0`. **Correct.** Linearity then makes `U` and `F + V` both `T`-valued solutions, so `lp:lm-uniqueness` forces `U = F` and `V = 0`. **No division by 2** — checked; this is the step where a careless argument would need `2` inverted.
- **The two maps.** `φ: l ↦ ia, m ↦ −iz` and `ψ: a ↦ −il, z ↦ im` send Laurent generators to units, hence define ring homomorphisms; all four round trips are the identity (`work/checks.py` C6). **Correct.**
- **`ψ(a^{-1}) = i l^{-1}`** — the step the printed proof singles out. **Correct** (`checks.py` C5).
- **Skein image.** Applying `ψ` to `aQ_+ − a^{-1}Q_- − zQ_0 = 0` and multiplying by the unit `i` returns the source skein. **Correct** (`checks.py` C3; the deliberately broken substitution `m ↦ +iz` fails, C3b).
- **Injectivity** of `R ⊂ R_G` closes the argument. **Correct.**

**What the lemma does and does not buy.** It buys uniqueness over `R` among
diagram-level maps that are Reidemeister-invariant. It does **not** remove an
external premise: it trades a ring quantifier no paper states for LM p. 120 (T2)
plus one page of campaign algebra (rule 8) plus **Reidemeister's theorem**,
which enters through `lp:lm-uniqueness`'s bridging sentence. SM6 states this
correctly at `sm-11:85–99`.

**A published cross-check, found this pass (rule 8).** LM **Prop. 10(ii)**
gives the obverse as the involution fixing `m` and swapping `l ↔ l^{-1}`. Pushed
through `φ`, that is `(a,z) ↦ (−a^{-1}, z)`. SM6's mirror paragraph
(`sm-3:4537`) instead uses `ι: (a,z) ↦ (a^{-1}, −z)`. **The two agree on every
monomial `a^p z^q` with `p ≡ q (mod 2)` and disagree off that locus** — and
`p ≡ q (mod 2)` for every monomial of the polynomial is exactly LM Prop. 22.
Verified exhaustively for `−8 ≤ p,q ≤ 8`, with the off-locus disagreement as
the negative control (`checks.py` C2, C2b). So the frame's internally derived
mirror formula and LM's printed symmetry are **consistent, not competing** —
a genuine corroboration of the transport chain, and the first one I know of that
ties the frame's mirror to a printed LM proposition. It is a consistency check,
**not** a proof of the transport, and the rule-8 flag stays.

## 5. The chain-shortening question (charter, F-25-54's note): LM Props 8, 9, 22

Asked: can LM Props 8, 9, 10, 22 shorten the campaign's chains by discharging
printed campaign lemmas by citation?

**Answer, deflated.** They shorten the *campaign's* chain and lengthen the
*source* reading, and they do not reduce the imported premise set at all:

- Props 8, 9 and 10 are all proved in LM §2 from the linear-skein module `L(P)`, whose **free** generation by the unknot class (p. 121) rests on §1's main theorem, **including its uniqueness clause**.

This is not an argument against citing them — a published proof still beats a
printed campaign derivation under rule 8, and SM6 already **records** them at

argument against describing them as a *reduction*. Two concrete cases:

- **`lem:homflyrows`** is available whole from Props 9, 8 and 22 at `c = 2`; SM6 says so and keeps its printed proof. Correct as printed. If it is ever switched to the citation, LM §2 comes with it.
